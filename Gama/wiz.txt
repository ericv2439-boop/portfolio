### Your `.65` device is not getting ARP replies for these addresses

For your machine:

```text
192.168.100.65
MAC e0:d5:5e:dc:56:17
```

I see repeated requests for:

| Target            | Requests | Reply visible? |
| ----------------- | -------: | -------------- |
| `192.168.100.5`   |     many | **No**         |
| `192.168.100.17`  |     many | **No**         |
| `192.168.100.91`  |     many | **No**         |
| `192.168.100.182` |     many | **No**         |

For example:

```text
GigaByteTech_dc:56:17 → Broadcast
Who has 192.168.100.17? Tell 192.168.100.65
```

and then it tries again.

Contrast that with `.83`:

```text
HuaweiTechno_72:a3:71 → Dell_da:e8:60
Who has 192.168.100.83? Tell 192.168.100.1

Dell_da:e8:60 → HuaweiTechno_72:a3:71
192.168.100.83 is at 48:4d:7e:da:e8:60
```

That's a **normal successful ARP exchange**.

### The really interesting part

Your `.65` machine repeatedly asks for `.5`, `.17`, `.91`, and `.182`, but there is **no corresponding `is at` response in the data you've shown**.

That means your computer is attempting to resolve those IPs to MAC addresses and apparently isn't learning them.

However, this **doesn't mean those devices are necessarily present and refusing to respond**. If they don't exist, are asleep, are isolated, or are on another segment, you'd get essentially the same behavior.

### You can verify this very cleanly

Put this in Wireshark:

```wireshark
arp && arp.src.proto_ipv4 == 192.168.100.65
```

Then switch to the **Packet Details** pane and inspect the ARP requests.

For specifically `.17`:

```wireshark
arp.dst.proto_ipv4 == 192.168.100.17
```

For `.5`:

```wireshark
arp.dst.proto_ipv4 == 192.168.100.5
```

For `.91`:

```wireshark
arp.dst.proto_ipv4 == 192.168.100.91
```

For `.182`:

```wireshark
arp.dst.proto_ipv4 == 192.168.100.182
```

If those filters show only:

```text
Who has X.X.X.X? Tell 192.168.100.65
```

with no:

```text
X.X.X.X is at XX:XX:XX:XX:XX:XX
```

then **your capture confirms the lack of ARP responses**.

One other thing stands out: the `.65` requests aren't random one-off ARPs. They're occurring in **clusters and recurring over the ~46-minute capture**, which makes it worth identifying **what process on your Windows machine is trying to reach those IPs**. That's the next thing I'd investigate.
